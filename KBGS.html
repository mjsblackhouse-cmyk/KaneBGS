Reading the Loss

The departure, reframed.

What it clarified

Losing him stings a little, and it should — you'd invested in him. But watch what happened when I asked what you actually need: you didn't reach for a like-for-like replacement. You named a different profile entirely. Someone with a national mindset, who documents and plans strategically, who empowers rather than fixes. Not the person who jumps on a truck to drive a vehicle across the state because operations is stretched.

That's not a consolation story. That's real clarity, earned the hard way. Worth writing down before it fades — the next hire, internal or external, should be built against that description, not against the shape of the person who just left.

Six Weeks

Designing the quiet.

Why this is the hardest thing you'll do

Not because it's difficult in the way work is difficult. Because it asks nothing of the part of you that's good at getting things done. A busy mind doesn't quiet on command — it takes real intention, not willpower.

1
Weeks one and two — shoulders down

Nothing short-cycle. No fixing, no checking, no quiet planning disguised as rest. Time with your wife and kids, and time alone, with no agenda attached to either.

2
The middle stretch — curiosity, not work

Follow what interests you, away from anything work-shaped. This is also where the harder question sits: who are you once you strip away achievement, growth, and the job? It's an old question — genuinely Aristotelian — and it's hard precisely because it's simple.

3
The final third — what you want to lean into

Once you're rested and you've sat with who you are outside the role, the question turns forward: what do you actually want the next chapter to hold?

If you want to improve a system, destabilise it. Coming back with the same rhythm, running the same plays, makes any change incremental. Coming back having decided you want something enough to change how you work — that's a different kind of return.

You'll be off email and Teams. That's the point, not a side effect — protect it fully rather than checking in "just once."

Watching From a Distance

The acquisition, as evidence.

Letting them run it

You caught yourself wanting to lean in, and chose not to. That's the exact behaviour this whole engagement has been working toward — and it's now happening on something real, with a board watching, not in a hypothetical. Worth debriefing properly once it resolves: what did the team do well without you, where did they need you and didn't have you, and what does that tell the board about how dependent Good Sammy currently is on you personally.

Beyond Good Sammy

What you offer, not just what you want.

The reframe

The useful question isn't what job you want next — it's what difference you make as an ingredient in a team. Particularly in the for-purpose sector, the sharper version is: how would this organisation be better with you in it? That's a different exercise from listing competencies, and it's one worth doing deliberately rather than answering on the spot in an interview.

Mock interviews will help — your own answers, said out loud, tend to sharpen your thinking faster than reflecting on paper. Worth booking these in as their own sessions, alongside a proper look at how you present on paper.
